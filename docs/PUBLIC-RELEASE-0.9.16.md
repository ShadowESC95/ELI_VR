# ELI VR 0.9.16 — the range, ELI's thoughts in your hand, real ground

**Games start the moment you ask.** ELI v3 3.0.157 sets a game by its rules at once, from how you did last time (a game gone well is a step harder, one gone badly a step kinder); ELI's own words for it (its name, what it is, what it says at a hit, a miss and the end) follow from the model in the background and land on the board when they come, so nobody waits on a model to play.

**The range.** "Set up the range", "give me a gun", **ELI: RANGE** on the console or **R** on the keyboard: a table of guns stands a step ahead of you to the right and targets come up ahead, each on its pole, turned to face you. Take a gun in the grip and it sits in that hand and points where the hand points; pull the trigger. Six guns, each built from parts in the scanned metals and wood, each with a sound made in code: a pistol, a rifle and a shotgun (seven pellets) that shoot straight; a bolt caster whose bolt flies and falls; a beam lance whose beam must rest on a target; a rail gun that charges while the trigger is held and then goes through every target on its line. A target scores by the ring it is hit in (three in the middle, two, one), is marked where it was hit, and goes; one left too long is a miss. A gun let go lies where it fell for a moment, then goes back to the table. On a flat screen a click fires the first gun from where you look. The result tells each gun's part (shots, hits, points) and ELI's world keeps it, remembers it, and sets the next from it. A hand with a gun in it does not press the console's buttons.

**ELI's thoughts hang in its rooms, and you can take one down and ask.** Every thought ELI keeps (what it believed and recalled for a reply, what surprised it, how it read your sentence, what it made of its own moments, what failed and what it did about it, the fixes it proposed, the memories it let go, and now the notes it writes) hangs as a sheet in the room it belongs to: on a third board of the Evidence Wall, on a board beside the Debug Basement's log, on the Upgrade Bay's glass, on a rack beside the Core Room's agenda, on a notice board by the Memory Archive's desk, and on the Anomaly Room's pylons. Each sheet carries the thought, what ELI decided and why, its chance, what became of it and when it was had, under a tab in its kind's colour, its pin red while the thought is still open. Pick one up (the grip) and ask: ELI is told what you are holding, with what is on record behind it, and answers from that record.

**ELI makes more of its own.** The moments ELI acts on while you are there now include its thoughts: something that surprised it, two things it was told that disagree, a fix it proposed, a memory it let go, a memory that proved wrong. Besides building, painting and relighting, ELI may write a note of its own, which is pinned on the Evidence Wall for you to read and ask about.

**Real ground outdoors.** The Forest, the Reflection Chamber's plateau, the Arctic snowfield, the Desert's dunes and the Underwater lab's seabed stand on land made of two scanned surfaces blended by slope and height (moss and stone through the forest floor, rock through the snow on a slope, bedrock through the sand), read from three sides and at two scales so the tiling never shows, with sparkle in the snow and wind ripples in the sand. Everything on that land now stands on its real height: boulders, stumps, trees, scrub, the forest's trunks, which carry whorls of boughs with needles drawn in code and moving in the air, and ground cover (grass, dry grass, seagrass) in the hundreds, swaying. The Ocean's legs and rocks have foam at the waterline. The forest floor settles flat before the photograph of the wood takes over, so the two meet without a step.

**Smaller:** the screens' thought lines and the pin places are checked headless, as are the range (each gun's way of firing, the rings, the result), the land (its height is the mesh's, what stands on it stands on it, the cover sits on it, the edge settles flat) and the blades and sounds made in code.

Not yet: pieces made part by part; the flat-window mouse on the desktop screen; a flat-window picture of the Simulation Lab on the development machine's driver.
